Why ISO 9 instead of "simple" translit?

Informal translit uses letter combinations such as "zh", "sh" or "ya". These are easy to type, but ambiguous: "sh" could be Ш or the letters С and Х. ISO 9 uses diacritics instead (ž, š, â), so every letter is reversible. That makes it ideal for libraries, academic texts and exact round-trips.

Hard and soft signs

The hard sign Ъ and the soft sign Ь have no sound of their own. ISO 9 keeps them as the modifier letters ʺ and ʹ, so that words like "объект" (obʺekt) can be restored to Cyrillic exactly.

Frequently asked questions

Is ISO 9 used in Russian passports?

No. ICAO Doc 9303 is used for passports or official transliteration. You can select it in the converter; ISO 9 is a separate reversible option.

Can I convert Latin back to Russian Cyrillic?

Yes. ISO 9 can convert Latin text back to Cyrillic, and GOST 7.79 System B supports back-conversion of correctly spelled words. Passport and official standards convert from Cyrillic to Latin only.

Does the converter work offline?

The web converter runs completely in your browser; your text is not sent to a server. The Android and iOS apps also work fully offline.
