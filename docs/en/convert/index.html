How Latin letters map to Cyrillic

        
Each language has its own rules. In Serbian, every Latin letter has exactly one Cyrillic counterpart, including the digraphs lj → љ, nj → њ and dž → џ, as well as đ → ђ, ć → ћ, č → ч, š → ш and ž → ж. Macedonian uses ѓ, ќ, ѕ and џ, and Montenegrin adds ś → с́ and ź → з́. For Russian, Ukrainian, Bulgarian and Belarusian, the converter uses the ISO 9:1995 standard, which maps every Cyrillic letter to exactly one Latin letter (e.g. щ → ŝ, ж → ž, я → â), so text can be converted back without loss.

        
Frequently asked questions

        
How do I type Cyrillic without a Cyrillic keyboard?

        
Type the text in Latin letters with your normal keyboard and select Latin to Cyrillic. The converter replaces each letter with its Cyrillic equivalent, so you can copy the result anywhere.

        
Does the converter handle digraphs like lj, nj and dž?

        
Yes. Serbian and Macedonian digraphs are recognized and converted to single Cyrillic letters such as љ, њ and џ, and back again.

        
Is my text uploaded when I convert it?

        
No. The converter runs locally in your browser via WebAssembly; your text never leaves your device.
